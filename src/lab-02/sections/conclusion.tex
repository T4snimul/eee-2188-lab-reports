\section{Conclusion}
\markright{Conclusion}

The V--I characteristics of a silicon p-n junction diode were studied in both forward and
reverse bias. In forward bias, the diode current was almost negligible at first and then
increased as the applied voltage was raised. Because a $10\,\text{k}\Omega$ series
resistor was used, the measured current stayed in the low-current range, and the forward
diode voltage was observed around $0.43$--$0.48\,\text{V}$ for the recorded conducting
points. This confirms the nonlinear forward characteristic of a silicon diode.

In reverse bias, the diode allowed only a very small leakage current, showing that it
acts approximately as an open switch before breakdown. The simulated results followed
the same trend as the observed readings and supported the theoretical operation of the
p-n junction diode.

Thus, the experiment successfully demonstrated diode polarity, cut-in voltage, reverse
saturation behavior, and the practical significance of the p-n junction diode as a
unidirectional conducting device.
