\section{Discussion}
\markright{Discussion}

This experiment showed that a single silicon diode can convert square, triangular,
and sinusoidal AC inputs into pulsating DC, but with limited efficiency and a high
ripple content. The measured results indicate that the square wave produced the highest
rectification efficiency, while the sinusoidal wave gave the lowest, because the waveform
shape changes the RMS value and the amount of AC energy left after rectification. The large
ripple factors also confirm that the simple half-wave circuit leaves substantial alternating
components in the output, so a smoother DC supply would require additional filtering or a
full-wave rectifier. The efficiency calculation was updated from the rough sheet to the main
report by utilizing the proper wave-specific equations in the conversion formula.
