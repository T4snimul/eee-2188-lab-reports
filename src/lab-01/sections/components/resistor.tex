\subsection{Resistor}
\markright{Resistor}

\subsubsection*{Definition}
A resistor is a passive, two-terminal electronic component that implements a defined
electrical resistance as a circuit element \cite{wiki:resistor}. It obeys Ohm's Law
($V = IR$), producing a voltage drop proportional to the current through it, and converts
electrical energy irreversibly into heat. Resistance is measured in ohms ($\Omega$). For
through-hole resistors, the resistance value and tolerance are encoded in a system of
colored bands printed around the component body (see Figure~\ref{fig:resistor_lab}): each
color represents a digit (0--9), a multiplier, or a tolerance percentage. Common band
systems use 4, 5, or 6 bands; the mnemonic ``Bad Beer Rots Our Young Guts But Vodka Goes
Well'' (Black, Brown, Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Blue, Violet, Grey, White) encodes the
digit order 0--9 \cite{electronics_notes:resistor}.

\subsubsection*{Specifications}
Key resistor specifications include: resistance value (m$\Omega$ to G$\Omega$), power
rating ($\frac{1}{8}$\,W to several hundred watts for industrial types), tolerance
($\pm0.1\%$ for precision metal-foil to $\pm20\%$ for general-purpose carbon composition),
and temperature coefficient of resistance (TCR)---how resistance drifts with temperature,
expressed in ppm/$^\circ$C \cite{electronics_notes:resistor}. Metal-film resistors achieve
TCR values of $\pm25$\,ppm/$^\circ$C, carbon-film types are typically
$\pm100$ to $\pm500$\,ppm/$^\circ$C, and carbon-composition resistors may drift
$\pm1000$\,ppm/$^\circ$C.

\subsubsection*{Purpose of Use}
The fundamental purpose of a resistor is to limit or regulate the flow of electric
current. Beyond this, resistors divide voltages, set the bias (operating point) of
transistors, establish the gain of amplifier stages, terminate transmission lines to
prevent reflections, and provide pull-up or pull-down functions on digital logic lines.

\subsubsection*{Applications}
Resistors are found in every electronic system without exception. They appear in
sensor conditioning networks, transistor biasing circuits, voltage dividers in ADC
front-ends, LED current-limiting circuits, audio equalizers, feedback networks around
operational amplifiers, and as load banks for power supply testing \cite{boylestad2015}.
